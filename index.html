01 — Dior & Saks advertising analysis

The assignment

I examined a photograph of a Dior and Saks outdoor display and used the idea of involuntary attention to explain why it could stand out to someone walking past.

My approach

I focused on the contrast between the bright decorations and the dark building, the large scale of the display, and the unusual combination of a blue circular centerpiece, flowers, butterflies, stars, and a moon. I connected these visible features to the possibility of noticing an advertisement before intentionally choosing to look at it.

What I learned

A display can catch someone’s eye through its visual design even before the person reads the brand name. I also learned to distinguish my interpretation of an image from evidence about actual customer behavior: this project did not measure purchases or advertising effectiveness.

Based on my coursework: “Involuntary Attention in the Dior and Saks Display.” Skills: visual observation, consumer behavior, and written analysis.

02 — Nonprofit fundraising article review

The assignment

I reviewed a predictive model of nonprofit fundraising and compared its focus on income and public awareness with research about donors’ concerns over overhead expenses.

My approach

I summarized the model, considered the limits of applying environmental-sector findings to other nonprofits, and discussed why a strong statistical fit does not automatically show how accurately a model will predict future donations. I then used the contrasting research to think about what a fundraising message should explain.

What I learned

Getting attention is only part of a fundraising campaign. A nonprofit also needs to explain what donations support. My recommendation was to use broad economic trends as a starting point and compare different messages against the organization’s own donation records. This was a literature review; I did not collect donor data or run a campaign.

Based on my coursework: “Article Review of Nonprofit Fundraising,” discussing Quevedo and Quevedo-Prince’s predictive model and Gneezy, Keenan, and Gneezy’s “Avoiding overhead aversion in charity.” Skills: secondary research, critical reading, and message evaluation.

03 — Forage interview preparation reflection

The exercise

I completed an interview preparation simulation and wrote a reflection on commercial awareness, motivation questions, and competency answers. One exercise used a fictional acquisition of wind and solar assets in several European countries.

My approach

I considered the transaction from the buyer’s, seller’s, lenders’, and regulators’ perspectives. I identified possible financing and approval concerns, then practiced connecting the important facts to a business decision. For interview answers, I used the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, and Result.

What I learned

A useful answer explains why a fact matters instead of repeating every detail. Comparing my work with an example helped me see where I needed to be more direct. I also recognized that I should spend more time explaining my own actions in a team project and keep practicing a clear, steady speaking pace.

Based on my “Forage Interview Preparation Job Simulation Reflection,” September 15, 2026. This was a virtual learning exercise. Skills: stakeholder analysis, reflection, and structured communication.
